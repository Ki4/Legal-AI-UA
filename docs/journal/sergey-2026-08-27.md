# 2026-08-27 — reading the schema out loud

No PR, and no code. A session spent explaining the repository to its owner, which turned out to be
a better review than a review.

## Reading fourteen migrations aloud found three things no gate had

Question by question, table by table, in the order they were built. Three defects surfaced only
because they had to be phrased for a person rather than checked against a rule:

- **`clients_select_staff` reads `jwt_role() in ('admin','lawyer')`.** Correct for one firm, where
  every member of staff is ours. The day registration opens to lawyers who are not — which is what
  tier 4 of the product needs — every registered lawyer reads every client anchor on the platform.
  Pseudonymous, so no name leaks; the volume and the timing do. About a dozen policies share the
  shape.
- **A fingerprint detects and does not reconstruct.** The register stores a hash of the normalized
  article and one hash at that, so the platform can say an article moved and never what it used to
  say. Agreed in the session to store the text; §9.7 now says so, before the fetcher that will be
  the only thing holding it.
- **The role is a single value, so a partner does not exist.** An admin cannot review an order and a
  lawyer cannot publish one. For a firm of three that is the normal case, not the exception. Q25.

None of the three is an error. Every gate was green throughout, every verification script passes,
and none of them would ever have gone red. Same class as the missing audit log in ADR-0010: not a
failure, silence.

## The product shape went first, deliberately

A second developer joins to own the generator. The instinct was to hand them a schema; what they
actually need is a contract, and a contract follows from what the product is. So the session wrote
`VISION.md` instead of code: four ways a need is met, of which the fourth is a lawyer rather than a
document; a provider is a listing and a tenant is an isolated workspace, and conflating them costs
months; a business client's documents are about people who are not the account; and what the MVP
proves, which is tier 1 and a chat.

Three of those turned out to need nothing new. The norm register is already shaped for many firms
because the dependency was split from the norm. The client account is already an organization
because the anchor holds no personal data. An external lawyer is already distinguishable from ours
by having no assignments. Twice in the session the answer to "how would we do that" was "it is
done", and that is worth recording as evidence that the earlier splits were cut along the right
lines rather than merely tidily.

## The STATE budget pushed content out for the first time

Three decisions taken in conversation did not fit in sixty lines. Previously that pressure produced
tighter sentences; this time it produced a correct move — the checklist of obligations went to §10,
the roles question became Q25, the norm text went into §9.7. The budget is supposed to force
something to close or relocate, and this is the first session where it forced relocation rather
than compression.

## What expired within the hour

`ls packages/ui/src/components/` said there was no Checkbox, no Switch, no Dialog. An hour later
there were, because the owner started building them while the conversation continued. The
observation was true when made and false when used — a small, cheap instance of exactly what the
"verified by hand" step of `/session-end` exists to catch, and the reason a check that lives only in
a transcript is not a check.
