# 2026-08-28e — a deferral that was careful and wrong, and three claims read against the schema

Fifth session of the day. PR #61 documents what changed on the anatomy screen; two things here have
no PR to live in, because neither produced one.

## A decision can be written well, argued honestly, and still name the wrong screen

`features/anatomy/api/types.ts` recorded, in a paragraph, why `selected_by` and `tool_calls` had no
view model: a view model exists because a screen renders it, these belong to the review screen, and
"they arrive here when the screen that renders them does — that is the layer working, not the layer
lagging." Every sentence of that is a good rule. The conclusion was still false, and `VISION.md` had
said so before either screen existed: the branching condition is _why a block is in this document_,
and this is the screen whose subject is that question.

What made it survive is that it reads as settled. The next session inherits a decision with its
reasoning attached and re-examines the reasoning, not the premise — the same shape as the debts of
2026-08-27c, which were phrased as diagnoses and stopped being re-opened for two weeks. The
difference worth naming: those were pessimistic ("no gate can read a `Depends` column"), this one
was tidy. Tidy is harder to notice.

## Two axes wearing one word

`BlockTrust` answers who wrote a block's text. Design-system §8.1 gives the `confirmed` marker a
different meaning — a block flips to it when a lawyer approves. Both documents are right; the console
mapped one onto the other and printed «Підтверджено юристом» over documents nobody had opened, on
the surface `VISION.md` calls the platform's flagship claim.

Nothing could have gone red. `check:copy` was not even looking at the file, and had it been, it
checks that a string went through `t()` and never which sentence was chosen. This is the class the
DoD names explicitly and cannot mechanise: "a green `check:copy` is evidence the mechanical half
holds, not that §6 is satisfied". The fix was one line of copy; finding it took reading the schema's
own description of the field beside the design system's description of the marker, which nothing
schedules.

The second axis was left as **Q27** rather than invented. It is not obviously the trace's: the trace
is the core's account of what produced a document and is archived unchanged in the passport
(ADR-0009), while an approval happens afterwards, in the console, and can happen twice.

## Three of VISION's "it is done" claims, read against the migrations

`2026-08-27` recorded that three product shapes needed nothing new. Asked to check them, and they
were checked against `supabase/migrations`, not against the journal that made the claims.

- **The norm register is shaped for many firms.** Half true, and the half that is missing is the one
  worth having found. The split is real — `law_norms` carries no firm, `service_law_refs` carries
  the dependency, writes there are assignment-gated. But `probe_interval`, `interval_reason` and
  `state` are _the watcher's judgement_ sitting on the row every watcher shares: `under_review` and
  `impact_confirmed` mean "somebody looked", and the policy lets a lawyer of one firm write them for
  everyone. It is a debt now, with the estimate attached — 46 references in one feature, nearly all
  behind its `api/` layer, and the triage screens that would multiply them are blocked on Q5–Q8, so
  the cheapest moment is the one where nothing is built on it yet.
- **The client account is already an organization.** The anchor holds no personal data, which was
  the claim's substance. But `client_identities` is keyed by `client_id` — one person per account —
  and the migration's own comment says membership is ADM-68. "Already" was doing more work than the
  schema supports.
- **An external lawyer is distinguishable by having no assignments.** True for writes. For reads,
  13 policies across 7 files are `jwt_role() in ('admin','lawyer')`, and no named predicate exists
  yet, which is exactly the precondition `VISION.md` sets before external registration opens.

The pattern across all three: the claims were made in prose, in a session with no code, and each was
accurate about the split it named and generous about what the split had finished. Reading the
migration is what separates the two, and it takes minutes.
