# 2026-09-18c — the close is the step that gets lost

Three PRs (#85–#87) document what changed. This is about how the session ended.

**Asked:** answer Q28, then put it in the schema.

**Done:** the ADR, two migrations, 51 new SQL scenarios; both applied to the cloud by hand, ledger
repaired, PR merged — and there the budget ran out, mid-sentence, before the explanation and before
`/session-end`.

**Learned:**

- The close is the cheapest step to skip and the most expensive to lose, because it is the only
  step the next session reads. STATE arrived three PRs behind and the briefing had to open with
  "trust git". Nothing was wrong; everything had to be re-derived from `gh pr view` and
  `migration list --linked` — ten minutes that a sixty-line rewrite would have saved.
- Repair the ledger, merge, close: the order was right and only the last step was missing. When
  the budget is visibly short, close **before** the merge — a merged PR with a stale STATE is
  worse than an unmerged PR with a current one, because the first reads as done.
- Seven fixtures broke when the sale began to require a signature. None of them was wrong when
  written; the rule moved. That is the runner's job, and the 2026-08-12 lesson — a fixture that
  publishes must publish the way the schema now demands — was carried by the runner and not by
  anyone remembering it.

**Abandoned:** nothing.
